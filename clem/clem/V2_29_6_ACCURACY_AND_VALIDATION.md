# v2.29.6 Accuracy and Validation

## Release purpose

v2.29.6 is a corrective release for the sea-ice physical-integrity defects found
in v2.29.5. The published hemispheric sea-ice area is now the model's native
thermodynamic area. A statistical intercept is no longer allowed to create or
remove published area. The remaining observation operator is restricted to a
continuous, monotone, zero-preserving 15% extent diagnostic.

## Evidence roles

1. **Development data:** 1979-2020 March and September NOAA/NSIDC sea-ice area
   records used to calibrate the native seasonal cycle and historical response.
2. **Validation-informed data:** 2021-2025 March and September records. These
   years were inspected during v2.29.5 development and are not described as an
   independent holdout.
3. **Prospective temporal test:** observations from 2027 onward are reserved
   before further tuning. March 2026 is reported separately because it was
   already inspected during correction work.
4. **Structural validation:** zero preservation, continuity, monotonicity,
   native-area identity, cap occupancy, conservation, timestep and resolution
   sensitivity, perturbation recovery, interface parity, worker safety and
   package integrity.
5. **External plausibility only:** the packaged OISST metadata is a broad
   open-water-temperature sanity reference. It is not treated as reproduced
   quantitative validation unless source bytes, hashes and processed output are
   present.

Development regressions and validation-informed records are not independent
predictive tests.

## Native sea-ice requirements

The release process evaluates native March and September area directly. It
checks the seasonal amplitude, calibration-period error, recent-period error,
historical September trend, scenario ordering and year-2100 behavior. The
published area and native thermodynamic area must agree to numerical precision.

The 15% extent mapping must satisfy:

- `extent(0) = 0`;
- continuity at zero;
- monotonic increase with native area;
- no positive intercept;
- a bounded extent-to-area ratio consistent with the 15% definition;
- no scenario- or calendar-time-dependent future closure.

## Thickness and winter-state requirements

The equivalent-thickness ceiling is an emergency numerical guard rather than a
calibration target. Validation reports maximum reference-cycle thickness,
ceiling occupancy and the fraction of the winter Arctic at the ceiling. A
release cannot pass if the reference climatology is clipping-dominated.

## AMOC and Arctic coupling

The default AMOC temperature-density coupling is restored to 1.00. The
low-confidence unresolved Arctic inversion/lapse-rate closure is set to
0.50 W/m²/K after native sea-ice recalibration so the model remains inside the
predeclared annual Arctic-amplification development range. This is an explicit
tuning-informed closure, not independent validation. Arctic surface and
shallow-ocean energy exchange remains equal and opposite within the resolved
heat budget. Positive transient hydraulic AMOC targets above the 17 Sv
control strength saturate smoothly toward 20 Sv, preventing the unphysical
25.5 Sv post-collapse rebound found in the 1,000-year 1200 ppm hold. This is a
low-confidence reduced hydraulic-drag/source-water closure; weakening and
reversal targets are unchanged. AMOC and Greenland outputs remain
reduced-complexity sensitivity diagnostics rather than precise forecasts.

## Reproducibility and release records

The authoritative numerical results are generated by `validate_v2296.py` and
stored in:

- `VALIDATION_SUMMARY_V2_29_6.json`;
- `DEEP_VALIDATION_V2_29_6.json`;
- `IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT_V2_29_6.json`;
- `TEST_RESULTS_V2_29_6.txt`.

The release is accepted only when every machine-generated release check passes,
the regression suite passes, and the extracted archive passes package-level
smoke verification.
